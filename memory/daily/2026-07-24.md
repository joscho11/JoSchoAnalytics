## 2026 opportunity and snap-input correction

The upcoming-board builder had two concrete deployment bugs. Vacated opportunity used a league-wide 2026 roster-membership set, so a player traded to another team was incorrectly treated as retained by his 2025 team; teams with no detected departure were omitted and became native missing values. `compute_vacated_opportunity` now compares each player's current team with his prior team, emits explicit zero rows, and normalizes ARI to AZ. Known-team 2026 coverage is now 100% for both vacated shares. The snap aggregator also failed to filter `game_type`, allowing postseason weeks into `prior_games` and per-game denominators; it now uses REG rows only. CMC therefore correctly has 17 prior games rather than 19.

The 2026 dataset and all QB/RB/WR/TE projection outputs were refreshed with the existing saved models; every model MD5 remained unchanged. McLaurin moved 69.0 to 127.9 (model WR27, matching ADP WR27), Evans 87.9 to 101.0, Pearsall 66.4 to 63.9, and CMC 118.5 to 144.7. The large McLaurin correction came from same-team vacated-share logic, not injury normalization. RB/WR/TE structural assembly passed; QB assembly stopped at its pre-existing +0.155 shuffle probe, so its veteran-only output was safely refreshed directly from the current dataset without weakening the assertion. The historical 2014-2025 CSV rows were intentionally not rebuilt; CSV round-tripping changed a small number of floating-point textual representations at roughly machine precision, so describe them as value-equivalent rather than byte-for-byte.

Two non-confirmatory, fixed-LightGBM walk-forward diagnostics used only 2021-2025 outcomes and no market columns. Replacing raw `prior_half_ppr` with `prior_ppg * 16.5` worsened pooled RB/WR/TE MAE by 0.09 points (33.79 to 33.88); on prior-games<=12 rows it improved only 0.06, while WR worsened 0.61. Do not deploy normalization. Rebuilding historical features with REG-only snap counts also worsened fixed-config MAE for QB/RB/WR/TE by +0.127/+0.868/+0.448/+0.111, despite correcting 354 deployment-era rows whose prior games exceeded the regular-season schedule. Do not retrain production models on that corrected history without a separately governed model-selection test. ## Global veteran projection-compression audit

A read-only audit joined all 2,589 veteran 2021–2025 walk-forward out-of-fold predictions to the corresponding historical feature rows with exact one-to-one coverage and no duplicate player-position-season keys. It used actual half-PPR totals only—no ADP or Sleeper evaluation. Pooled predicted-minus-actual bias was -3.72 points versus 34.73 MAE. By position it was QB -9.90, RB -7.70, TE -2.57, and WR +0.53.

The preregistered-in-chat primary diagnostic selected the top predicted quintile within position-season, avoiding the mechanical underprediction created by selecting on realized outcomes. Top-tier bias was QB +16.29, RB -21.33, TE -1.58, and WR +4.15 points. Only RB had a player-cluster bootstrap interval excluding zero (-35.51 to -5.70); its leave-one-season-out bias remained negative regardless of which season was removed. That RB slice was discovered within a multi-position diagnostic scan, so it is a future hypothesis rather than accepted evidence for changing production.

The apparent cross-position pattern is availability uncertainty: every position overpredicted players who later missed substantial time and underpredicted players who later stayed healthy. This future-conditioned split is explanatory only and cannot be used at forecast time. Critically, WRs coming off 12 or fewer games with top-quartile prior PPG were overprojected by 21.31 points on average (n=45; player-cluster 95% interval +1.26 to +39.48). Therefore a blanket WR uplift or shortened-season normalization would make the measured class worse. Pearsall remains an individual miss if healthy, not evidence of global WR compression. No production model, feature set, or projection artifact changed.
