# Graphs of the Week 01: NFL Week 3

*2026 · Week 3 · four stats worth a closer look*

**The one-liner:** A new recurring format. Each week I take one strange, viral-sounding stat and put a chart around it to see whether it holds up. This week: Joe Burrow's reported 1-34 record, Week 3's oddly competitive Sunday slate, three 0-3 teams that started with high win totals, and the Chargers losing to Buffalo despite forcing 5 turnovers.

---

## 1. Joe Burrow's reported 1-34 record

The viral number is Burrow going 1-34 when Cincinnati trails in the final 8 minutes of a game. That counts final score outcomes, which buries the part where the offense actually did its job.

Narrowing to drives starting in the final 8 minutes, team down 1 to 8 points, Burrow at QB, 2020-2025: Cincinnati tied or took the lead on **7 of 17** such possessions, **41%**. The 46-quarterback peer median over the same window was **35%** — Burrow is above it, not a laughingstock.

Other QBs charted on the same stat: Brady 8/12 (67%), Mahomes 8/25 (32%), Josh Allen 13/25 (52%), Zach Wilson 4/14 (29%), and Deshaun Watson second-lowest at 2/17 (12%).

A tie-or-lead isn't a final win, so this doesn't erase the 1-34 record. It does show the record is measuring something closer to bad luck and a leaky defense than bad clutch quarterbacking.

---

## 2. Week 3's oddly competitive Sunday

On Sunday of Week 3, **12 of 14 games (86%)** finished within one score (8 points or less). Across the 2018-2025 Sunday sample, the median one-score rate is **54%**, and the previous high was **82%**. This Sunday set a new high.

No mechanism is claimed here — it's flagged on screen as a genuine outlier with no clear cause, not a trend.

---

## 3. The 0-3 teams that started with real win totals

Three teams sit at 0-3 this year despite opening with a preseason win total of 8.5 or higher: the Texans, Chargers, and Buccaneers.

Charting preseason win total against final regular-season wins for every team 2014-2025, color-coded by playoff result: only **9 team-seasons** in that 12-year span even qualify for "high preseason total, winless through 3," and only **2 of those 9** made the playoffs — one being last year's Texans. No year in the sample had more than 2 teams qualify at once; this year has 3.

It's not unprecedented for one of these teams to make the playoffs, but the base rate (2 of 9) says it's a real uphill climb, not just a hot-take that they're dead.

---

## 4. Chargers force 5 turnovers, still lose to Buffalo

The Chargers forced 5 Buffalo turnovers and lost by 8 points anyway. Looking at every regular-season game 2018-2025 with at least 5 giveaways and a turnover margin of -4 or worse (38 games): **37 losses, 1 tie, 0 wins**. The median margin in those games was -15.5.

Buffalo's game sits alone as the single winning data point in that 38-game sample. The takeaway spoken on screen is blunt: turning the ball over that badly and still winning is close to a league-wide impossibility, which makes the Chargers' performance around it look worse, not better.

---

## 5. What this does not prove

- **Burrow section doesn't erase the record.** A tie-or-lead is not a win; the 1-34 figure is still real, just measuring a narrower thing than "Burrow chokes."
- **The Sunday stat has no identified cause.** It's an outlier callout, not an explanation.
- **The 0-3 teams could still make the playoffs.** 2 of 9 historically did. Small sample either way.
- **The Chargers loss doesn't prove Buffalo is bad or lucky.** It's one data point standing alone in a 38-game historical set; it says the win is rare, not undeserved.
- All four charts use 2014-2025 or 2018-2025 windows only — nothing here is a full-history claim.
