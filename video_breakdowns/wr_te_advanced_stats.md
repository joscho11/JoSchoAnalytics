# WR and TE Advanced Stat Leaders, Weeks 1-2

*2026 · Weeks 1-2 · receivers and tight ends in one pool, 6 advanced stats*

**The one-liner:** Wide receivers and tight ends ranked together on six advanced stats through two weeks: yards per route run, average depth of target, WOPR, yards after the catch, first downs per route and a catch-rate success measure. It is two games of public data, and several of these boards are led by players who barely cleared the minimum.

---

## 1. How the boards are built

- **Source:** nflverse 2026 play-by-play through Week 2 (all 32 games), plus public snap counts. Snapshot taken 2026-09-22.
- **One pool.** WRs and TEs compete on the same boards.
- **Garbage time:** every play is dropped when either team's win probability is above 90% before the snap, for every stat.
- **Rates come from summed counts** across both weeks, not an average of the two weekly rates.
- **Sorting** uses the unrounded value. Ties go to the larger sample, then to the name.
- **Minimums:** 20 route-proxy snaps for yards per route and first downs per route, 8 targets for depth of target and success rate, 20 route-proxy snaps plus 6 targets for WOPR, 6 receptions for yards after the catch.

The route count is a proxy, not a tracked number: a player's offensive snaps, scaled to the team's pass plays that survived the garbage-time filter. First-read target share is not included.

## 8. What this does not prove

- **That any of these players is elite or has fallen off.** Several leaders are on 8 to 20 targets or routes. Davante Adams leads yards per route on 20.9 route-proxy snaps, barely above the 20 minimum.
- **That the route counts are real.** They are scaled snaps. Play-level route data would move some of these numbers.
- **That depth of target or catch rate measures skill.** ADOT mostly describes how a player is used, and a catch rate rewards short targets.
- **That the list carries forward.** These are cumulative through Week 2. They are not projections.
